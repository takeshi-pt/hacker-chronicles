~/about

An old computer. A lot of curiosity.

Somehow, I'm still figuring things out. And enjoying the process.

Hello, I'm Nunex.

I'm a long-time Linux user, computer enthusiast and beginner programmer.

I've been messing around with computers for most of my life, although learning to write software properly is a much more recent adventure.

I'm mostly self-taught. My maths skills are questionable, my curiosity is not, and I have a habit of turning small experiments into unnecessarily complicated projects.

But that's half the fun.

How it all started

My first computer was a 486 back in the 1990s. I was about twelve years old, completely fascinated by MS-DOS, games and figuring out how things worked.

At one point, I even deleted Windows 95 to make more room for games.

Priorities. 😄

Life eventually took me away from computers for a while, but the interest never really disappeared.

In my early twenties, I discovered Linux because I wanted more control over how my computer worked. Ubuntu was the beginning, followed by years of exploring different distributions, desktop environments and ways of doing things.

Linux became much more than an operating system to me. It became a playground.

Eventually, all that tinkering led me back to programming.

I'd tried learning to code before, but never quite managed to stick with it. This time feels different. I have more patience, better learning tools, and a genuine interest in understanding how things work.

There's also something surprisingly peaceful about solving a programming problem.

When I'm deep in a problem, everything else quiets down.
That's worth a lot to me.

So, what is Hacker Chronicles?

Hacker Chronicles is my little corner of the internet.

Part technical notebook, part experimental workshop, part collection of things I find interesting.

You'll find Linux adventures, configuration experiments, programming notes, little command-line tools, games, ASCII art, and occasionally something completely unexpected.

I'm currently learning Go, exploring software development, and discovering that knowing how to use Linux for twenty years doesn't automatically mean knowing how to program!

The idea isn't to pretend I'm an expert. Quite the opposite.

I want somewhere to document what I learn, share things that work, laugh about things that don't, and maybe create something useful or entertaining along the way.

This isn't a professional publication or a tutorial factory.

Things appear when they're ready. Sometimes they don't. That's fine too.

Humans, robots and artificial intelligence

AI has become part of how I learn and create.

I use ChatGPT to explore ideas, explain concepts, help with programming, improve my writing and occasionally produce something completely ridiculous.

The website itself was built with AI assistance, using HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript.

English isn't my first language, so having help turning my ramblings into something readable is particularly useful.

I come up with the ideas, make the decisions and explore the results. ChatGPT handles a suspiciously large amount of the actual typing.

It's a rather productive arrangement.

At some point, this arrangement also produced Takeshi: The Lazy Dev, a fictional software developer who delegates everything to AI, takes all the credit, and somehow keeps becoming more successful.

That particular experiment has already escaped containment.

You can visit Takeshi's ridiculous little universe elsewhere on this site. The stories are satire, the studio is fictional, but some of the software projects are very real.

One last thing

I'm not here to chase numbers, follow trends or promise regular content.

I like old computers, lightweight software, open-source projects, things that run in terminals, and the feeling of finally understanding something that confused me yesterday.

Mostly, I'm here to learn and have fun doing it.

Welcome to Hacker Chronicles.

Made with curiosity, too much coffee, and a little help from AI. ♥
