SAGA 001 · THE AFTERFALL INCIDENT

How to build a game without doing any work.

A condensed, satirical history inspired by how a small Go learning project unexpectedly became an actual text RPG and then a full-screen Bubble Tea experiment.

THE LEGEND

“I need a full beta-ready game. Don't let me down.”

— Takeshi, immediately before delegating all programming
THE REALITY

The milestones are based on our real development sessions. The office politics, millions, interviews and exaggerated company crises are fictional comedy.

The Beta 3 source will be published after a proper technical review.

ACT 01 / THE FIRST COMMIT

It started with a robot and a welcome screen.

A curious hobbyist learning Go writes a tiny post-human robot adventure. There are a couple of files and some ASCII output. This is supposed to be a learning exercise.

ACT 02 / EXECUTIVE DECISION

“Build the entire game.”

Takeshi role-plays as a lazy game developer facing a deadline. ChatGPT gets one sweeping creative assignment: lore, enemies, NPCs, quests, saves and endings. A complete playable beta appears.

ACT 03 / UNEXPECTED SUCCESS

Beta 1 launches, and the cat survives.

The text RPG runs. Players can roam, fight, help PIP, speak to MIRA, restore the relay and decide the fate of the Choir. Takeshi requests a promotion.

ACT 04 / UI MARKETING

Bubble Tea enters the boardroom.

The company promises a dashboard with separate story, map and inventory panels. On narrower terminals, the interface collapses into one big box. The memes are immediate.

ACT 05 / RELEASE ENGINEERING

One missing dependency checksum.

A real go.sum error briefly prevents startup. The AI prescribes go mod tidy. The CEO calls it a major infrastructure breakthrough.

ACT 06 / CRISIS RESOLVED

Six panels. Real dashboard. A very happy CEO.

Beta 3 corrects the compact interface. The imaginary stock price rises. QA asks for paid leave; the CEO launches a marketing campaign instead.

ACT 07 / EXPANSION

The Rust Quarter Times is founded.

The fictional studio becomes the seed of a comic/news universe. The project gains a face, a banner and its own area inside Hacker Chronicles.

A story based on real creative software experiments, told as exaggerated fiction. The game source is available to inspect; the studio’s corporate drama is invented.
